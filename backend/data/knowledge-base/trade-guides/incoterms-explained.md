# Incoterms 2026 - Complete Guide

## Overview

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) are globally recognized trade terms that define the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in international trade. Published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC), Incoterms 2020 provides clear rules for delivery, risk transfer, and cost allocation.

## Why Incoterms Matter

- **Clear Risk Transfer**: Defines when risk passes from seller to buyer
- **Cost Allocation**: Specifies who pays for what
- **Documentation**: Standardizes documentation requirements
- **Dispute Prevention**: Reduces misunderstandings
- **Global Standard**: Recognized in 180+ countries

## The 11 Incoterms 2020

### Group 1: Any Mode of Transport

#### EXW - Ex Works
- **Seller's Obligations**: Make goods available at their premises
- **Buyer's Obligations**: All transportation, customs clearance, costs, and risks
- **Risk Transfer**: At seller's premises
- **Best For**: Experienced buyers, domestic shipments
- **⚠️ Caution**: Buyer bears all risks; not recommended for inexperienced traders

#### FCA - Free Carrier
- **Seller's Obligations**: Deliver goods to carrier at named place
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Main carriage, insurance, costs beyond delivery
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods delivered to carrier
- **Best For**: Containerized cargo, multimodal transport

#### CPT - Carriage Paid To
- **Seller's Obligations**: Pay for carriage to named destination
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Insurance, import clearance, onward transport
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods delivered to first carrier
- **Best For**: Multimodal transport with seller paying freight

#### CIP - Carriage and Insurance Paid To
- **Seller's Obligations**: Pay for carriage AND insurance to destination
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Import clearance, any additional costs
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods delivered to first carrier
- **Best For**: High-value goods needing insurance

#### DAP - Delivered at Place
- **Seller's Obligations**: Deliver goods on arriving transport at named place
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Unload goods, import clearance, duties
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods available at named place on transport
- **Best For**: Door-to-door delivery, buyer handles import

#### DPU - Delivered at Place Unloaded (NEW in Incoterms 2020)
- **Seller's Obligations**: Deliver AND unload goods at named place
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Import clearance, duties, onward transport
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods unloaded at named place
- **Best For**: Terminal delivery, port deliveries (replaces DAT)

#### DDP - Delivered Duty Paid
- **Seller's Obligations**: All costs including customs clearance and duties
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Unload goods, any inspections
- **Risk Transfer**: At buyer's premises (door)
- **Best For**: Buyer wants no involvement in import process

### Group 2: Sea and Inland Waterway Transport Only

#### FAS - Free Alongside Ship
- **Seller's Obligations**: Deliver goods alongside vessel at named port
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Loading, main carriage, insurance, export clearance
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods alongside ship
- **Best For**: Bulk cargo, port-to-port shipments

#### FOB - Free on Board
- **Seller's Obligations**: Deliver goods ON BOARD vessel at named port
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Main carriage, insurance, import clearance
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods on board vessel
- **Best For**: Port-to-port sea freight, most common incoterm

#### CFR - Cost and Freight
- **Seller's Obligations**: Pay for carriage to destination port
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Insurance, import clearance, onward transport
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods on board vessel
- **Best For**: Sea freight, seller pays freight

#### CIF - Cost, Insurance and Freight
- **Seller's Obligations**: Pay for carriage AND insurance to destination
- **Buyer's Obligations**: Import clearance, duties, onward transport
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods on board vessel
- **Best For**: Sea freight, high-value goods, insurance included

## Which Incoterm Should You Choose?

### For Sellers (Exporters)
- **Want Minimum Risk**: Use DDP or CIF
- **Want Simplicity**: Use CIF or CIP
- **Want Control**: Use DAP or DPU
- **Avoid If New**: Use EXW or FAS

### For Buyers (Importers)
- **Want Maximum Control**: Use EXW
- **Want Minimum Risk**: Use DDP
- **Want Low Cost**: Use FOB or FCA
- **Avoid If New**: Use DAP or DPU

## Common Misunderstandings

### ❌ FOB vs FCA Confusion
- FOB is ONLY for sea freight
- FCA is for ANY mode of transport

### ❌ "Free" Means No Cost
- "Free" means seller is FREE of responsibility
- Buyer pays, not "free" as in no charge

### ❌ CPT vs CIP
- CPT: Seller pays freight ONLY
- CIP: Seller pays freight AND insurance

## Best Practices

✅ **Always Specify Incoterms in Contracts**: Include city/port and version (Incoterms 2020)
✅ **Be Specific**: "FOB Shanghai Port" not just "FOB"
✅ **Consider Insurance**: CIF and CIP include insurance
✅ **Know Your Responsibilities**: Understand seller vs buyer obligations
✅ **Document Everything**: Incoterms affect pricing and risk

## Examples

### Example 1: Textile Importer from India
- **Product**: Cotton fabric from China
- **Volume**: 1 FCL (full container load)
- **Incoterm**: FOB Shanghai
- **Seller's Responsibility**: Delivery to port, export clearance
- **Buyer's Responsibility**: Freight, insurance, import clearance
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods on board vessel in Shanghai

### Example 2: Machinery Buyer in Germany
- **Product**: Industrial machinery from Japan
- **Value**: €200,000
- **Incoterm**: CIF Hamburg
- **Seller's Responsibility**: Freight and insurance to Hamburg
- **Buyer's Responsibility**: Import clearance, duties
- **Risk Transfer**: When goods on board vessel in Japan

### Example 3: Consumer Electronics to USA
- **Product**: Smartphones from Vietnam
- **Volume**: 100 cartons
- **Incoterm**: DDP Chicago
- **Seller's Responsibility**: Everything including US duties
- **Buyer's Responsibility**: Unload and receive goods
- **Risk Transfer**: At buyer's warehouse
